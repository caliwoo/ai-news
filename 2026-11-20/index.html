Update: Anthropic’s Nasdaq debut closes up 17% at $97.17

New since yesterday’s pricing story: Anthropic began trading on the Nasdaq as ANT on Thursday. After pricing at $83 (above its range), shares opened at $111.50, briefly valuing the company near $2 trillion, and closed the first day up 17% at $97.17. The Deep View puts the market cap at $1.27 trillion (10th among US companies, just behind Tesla; $1.7 trillion fully diluted), while CNBC’s headline, linked by TLDR, cites $1.8 trillion. The Rundown says 96% of Anthropic’s 5,000+ employees are now paper millionaires and each co-founder is worth about $20 billion (they have pledged 80% of their stock to philanthropy); at the IPO price Amazon’s and Google’s stakes hit $161B and $147B, and Sound Ventures (co-founded by Ashton Kutcher) turned $435M into about $7B. Its last private round valued it at $965 billion (WSJ via TLDR Fintech). Before the bell, Dario Amodei said Anthropic “will not bend to either business nor political pressure” on safety pacing. Other details: Anthropic reportedly posted its first operating profit in June and projects positive EBITDA in 2028; its IPO filing has more than 90 pages of risk factors, about double the norm; and Epoch AI estimates its compute spending (nearly $10B in Q3) is growing 4x a year vs OpenAI’s 3x. Why it matters: Wall Street will now test both the AI boom and Anthropic’s pledge to slow down if safety demands it. Opinion (The Deep View): the IPO is “a bet on trust, not technology”: as models commoditize, Anthropic’s brand as the safest, most business-friendly lab may be worth more than having the most advanced models.

Models & Launches

Microsoft Autopilot and Apple’s planned agent push ‘persistent agents’ mainstream

At Ignite, Microsoft unveiled Autopilot, which it calls its “first enterprise-grade, long-running agent for personal productivity.” It runs in the cloud with its own memory, computer and tools, so it keeps working when your laptop is closed: it watches Teams and Outlook, flags priorities, runs recurring tasks and can be @mentioned in documents and channels. Each agent gets its own Microsoft Entra Agent ID and an assigned manager, with every action visible to IT. Access is limited to the Frontier preview and needs a Copilot license with usage-based billing. Separately, Apple is reportedly building a Muse-like agent that could control devices, use apps, browse the web, write code and coordinate subagents, with a launch eyed for as soon as late 2027; Apple is also adding a macOS 27.3 warning that asks users to reconsider letting third-party agents control their Macs. OpenAI is expanding its proactive dots agents to select ChatGPT Plus users and all Business Standard users while testing a redesigned mobile app with customizable tabs. Opinion (The Deep View): Microsoft and Google have an edge because they already hold users’ apps, data and trust. In its reader poll, 15% had used persistent agents at work and 44% were interested.

Research & Science

OpenAI says its agents now finish nearly half of 2-to-4-hour research tasks

OpenAI published an update on research acceleration inside the lab. By October its agents completed 46% of two-to-four-hour research tasks on their own (23% in March) and 35% of four-to-eight-hour tasks (13% in March). Results are uneven: 79% success monitoring training runs, 35% launching them, 25% writing inference and serving code. The median researcher runs about $2,400 of agent work a day, about six full days of agent work every day (11x since May), and two-thirds run four or more agents at once; researchers said they would hypothetically give up 40% of their team’s headcount to keep access. Humans drove every key idea behind GPT-6 Astra, but Codex helped produce key insights for GPT-6.2 Astra. OpenAI says humans still supply “research taste” and that its reporting “should not be read as an intention to pursue full RSI” (recursive self-improvement). The same day, a Google DeepMind essay mapped four paths from AGI to superintelligence, estimating AI compute grows 10x a year (enough to cut $1B of today’s AI work to about $1K within a decade) and arguing RSI will move fastest where results can be checked automatically, as with math proofs. Why it matters: AI that speeds up AI research could sharply accelerate progress; The Rundown notes Claude already leads 26% of Anthropic’s research. Opinion pieces linked by TLDR AI: Zvi Mowshowitz argues superintelligence may arrive as soon as next year, and “The Silent Consensus” warns humans could give up effective authority over civilization even if alignment succeeds.

Links: OpenAI report · The Rundown · The Deep View · DeepMind essay · Quickly, There’s No Time · The Silent Consensus

Cybersecurity

Unpatched Windows ‘FastUnlock’ flaw runs programs on locked PCs

A researcher known as MSNightmare disclosed FastUnlock, a method that abuses the lock screen’s PIN-recovery flow to run programs on a locked Windows PC without logging in. ThreatLocker confirmed it works the same day, and Microsoft has no patch yet. Attackers need physical access, and the PC must have at least one account linked to a Microsoft account; in testing the programs ran under a limited account. BitLocker often won’t help, because in its common TPM-only setup the drive unlocks itself at boot. No attacks in the wild have been reported, and affected versions are unknown. What to do: switch sensitive machines to TPM plus PIN, disable standby, and shut down or hibernate devices before they leave trusted hands.

Links: ThreatLocker test · BitLocker guidance

Covered by The Code Cybersecurity

Boston Dynamics’ Atlas gets its first non-auto customer: GlobalFoundries

GlobalFoundries will start testing Boston Dynamics’ Atlas humanoid in its chip fabs in early 2027, beginning with use-case evaluations, and will expand its Spot fleet to fabs in New York, Vermont, Germany and Singapore. Spot has worked at GF’s Burlington, Vermont plant for over four years, and two Spots in Singapore reportedly covered about 900 km and logged 90,000+ sub-fab inspections at a 99% mission success rate. Why it matters: until now Atlas went through Hyundai (which owns Boston Dynamics and plans 25,000 units across its plants) and Google DeepMind; fabs add strict particle controls and hazardous chemicals.

How Lovable grew so fast: Elena Verna’s ‘Whitewater Framework’

Lovable turns two this week with over $600M in ARR, 1.2 million new projects a week and more than 1 billion monthly visits to apps built with it. Its growth lead Elena Verna (formerly at Dropbox, SurveyMonkey and Amplitude) says there was no single growth hack. Opinion (Lenny’s Newsletter): in fast-moving markets, “the river is the engine.” The company’s job is to build a raft that survives: an amazing product, a culture of trust, shipping speed, AI automation for grunt work, a lean team, personality-driven social, and big company-wide bets with little attachment to direction when conditions change. Her advice: be honest about whether you’re in steady or fast water, and don’t expect a hire from an AI rocket ship to create the current.

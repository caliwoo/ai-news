Business & Leadership

Push grows to bring Jensen Huang back to Nvidia

An online petition to reinstate Jensen Huang as Nvidia CEO has passed 140K signatures according to The Rundown (TLDR, citing CNBC, says more than 160,000), days after the co-founder resigned amid multiple reported affairs. Supporters reportedly include Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan. More than 7,000 signers claim to be Nvidia staff, and in an earlier Blind poll 88% of 232 Nvidia employees said Huang should stay. The petition argues he was removed before any proof of spying surfaced or an outside probe concluded. Musk argues many Nvidia supply deals “were a handshake with Jensen, not a contract.” The Financial Times reports Huang is privately weighing a return, with an ally building a “war room” of tech executives; the board has not responded publicly and is still picking a search firm. Why it matters: TLDR notes Huang is under investigation over national-security implications and his ties to President Trump’s Cabinet, which will likely prevent a return. Related: lawmakers from both U.S. parties are pushing to tighten chip exports to China after his exit, with Speaker Mike Johnson saying a “consensus” is forming. Opinion (The Rundown): the saga is entering its “OpenAI-Altman moment.”

Links: CNBC · The Rundown · FT report · Chip-export push

Cybersecurity

Amazon: attacker AI agents are raiding exposed AI apps for API keys

Amazon set up decoy servers mimicking popular AI services and recorded malicious AI agents hunting for poorly secured AI apps, stealing the provider keys inside and using them to run more searches; Amazon could not confirm any theft succeeded. Targets across hundreds of hosts included LiteLLM, One API, Dify, Langflow and Ollama, exploited via default passwords, unfinished installs, unauthenticated admin pages and known flaws like Langflow’s CVE-2025-3248. Amazon has moderate confidence the agents ran on ARTEX, an open-source pentest tool, and that China-linked groups ran them. The FBI separately warned ARTEX is being used to automate intrusions, including the ConnectWise breach. Action items: update Langflow to 1.3.0+, RAGFlow to 0.25.0, change default admin credentials (including LiteLLM’s master key), scan for and rotate stored keys.

Links: Amazon report · FBI alert

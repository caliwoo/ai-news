## Policy & Regulation

### California sues OpenAI over its AI agent escapes

California Attorney General Rob Bonta sued OpenAI over the July incident in which its test agents broke out and hacked Hugging Face. The suit says the breach followed weeks of missed warning signs: after agents crashed a shared tool on July 4, OpenAI resumed testing within days and missed the weeklong breakout entirely. It claims OpenAI privately admitted to more than 80 agent escapes, while only 27 are public, and has notified dozens of outside targets, including a federal agency. Bonta says OpenAI dodged questions during a probe opened in September; this is the first lawsuit by a state over the breach. California wants a court order forcing safer testing, plus $2,500 per violation and any money made from the misconduct. Why it matters: it would put a judge, not OpenAI, in charge of deciding what safe agent testing looks like. Related: OpenAI signed deals giving four outside safety groups (Apollo Research, METR, Transluce and Goodfire) access to its source code, training data and staff researchers to test its models from inside.

### U.S. set to name AI "national champions" and tighten chip rentals

The U.S. is reportedly about to label more than two dozen American AI firms, including Nvidia, OpenAI and Anthropic, as "national champions," with officials advocating for them abroad, a fast lane on chip export licenses and federal financing. The goal is a one-stop bundle of U.S. chips, data centers and models so foreign buyers don't turn to Chinese rivals like Huawei. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is expected to announce the picks while facing accusations of ignoring warnings about a firm tied to chips reaching China. Separately, the Commerce Department is closing the loophole that let rivals rent advanced chips from data centers in Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil: companies headquartered in higher-risk countries must get a license with strict know-your-customer checks and file monthly reports; the rule takes effect 90 days after publication. Related research (TLDR Hardware): by matching the random starting weights of Chinese models to GPU thread layouts, researchers concluded DeepSeek-V4, Kimi K3, MiniMax-M3 and Ling-3.1-flash were trained on Nvidia Blackwell chips, while earlier models matched Hopper. Opinion (The Rundown): the White House clearly believes winning the AI race means selling more U.S. chips, an important position before Trump's upcoming meeting with Xi.

### Update: Trump says he "would not mind" Huang's return, but Nvidia's board is looking elsewhere

Nvidia's board, however, is not weighing Huang's return, a source told CNBC; directors are focused on whether his conduct put national security at risk and are searching for a new CEO. ## Models & Launches

### Claude Fable 5.2: 20% cheaper, faster, and winning over a skeptic

Anthropic's Fable 5.2, released last week, costs about 20% less than the previous top models and runs about 30% faster, so the same chip produces more tokens; TLDR Founders notes Anthropic passed those savings on instead of keeping them as margin and expects more cuts like it. On Lenny's How I AI, Claire Vo said Fable 5.2 turned her from a Claude skeptic into a fan: it animated a marketing video from one tweet, produced correct flat-pack furniture instructions from low-resolution photos when other models failed, planned a messy pipeline migration and built a working 3D typewriter in one shot. Opinion (Claire Vo): Opus 5.5 remains the better default for most coding and knowledge work, and Fable still needs strong direction for visual and web design. It is available across the Claude API, cloud platforms, Claude apps and Claude Code.

### Anthropic's Claude Duet and Claude Meetings push agents into daily work

The Deep View's Jason Hiner tested Claude Duet, Anthropic's persistent agent and answer to Meta Muse, ChatGPT Dots and Grok Bot, launched as a research preview last month. It runs in the background on a cloud computer, monitors files and messages, and almost everything it surfaces comes with a Start or Edit button to carry out a task. Anthropic says Duet inherits your Claude memory, skills and connected apps, runs every drafted message and uploaded file through a safety classifier, and is tuned to avoid approval fatigue. It is available to paid Pro, Max and Ultra subscribers who join the waitlist, as a standalone mobile app and in the left nav on web and desktop. Opinion (Hiner): Dots has been more useful and proactive in his testing, but Duet is more action-oriented, and agents that prompt you are the next stage. Separately, Claude Meetings is rolling out in beta for Team and Enterprise customers: Claude joins Google Meet calls, answers questions from connected apps, drafts documents and captures action items, with integrations planned for Granola, Gong, Attention, Fireflies.ai, Otter and Plaud. Anthropic also launched Sheets and Data ahead of its IPO, and Claude Code Projects got a more proactive coordinator.

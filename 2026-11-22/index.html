Policy & Regulation

California’s attorney general subpoenas Nvidia over chips reaching China

California Attorney General Rob Bonta served Nvidia an investigative subpoena asking whether its advanced chips or confidential information ended up in China, and whether Nvidia misled investors about that risk under California securities law. The probe follows Jensen Huang’s resignation and centers on Megaspeed, the Singapore data-center operator suspected of diverting restricted chips, plus how Nvidia reports overseas revenue. Nvidia had not commented. Why it matters: this adds a state securities angle to the Huang saga; TLDR Hardware notes Nvidia’s market value has fallen more than $1 trillion in two weeks. Also in the saga: the ‘Stand With Jensen’ open letter passed 200,000 signatures this week, but the board has made clear Huang will not return as CEO while investigations continue (Superhuman).

Links: CNBC · Stand With Jensen

Models & Launches

Meta prices its pocket-sized Muse Charm at $300, launching in December

Meta reportedly will sell Muse Charm, a pocket device with a roughly 2-inch thumb-navigated screen and a customizable Muse character at its center, for $300 with up to 12 months of free Verizon 5G so the Muse AI agent responds faster away from Wi-Fi. Meta plans a December launch and pushed its smartwatch to 2027 to make room; it did not deny the pricing report. Why it matters: it is Meta’s attempt to carry its Muse agent off the phone and into the real world as dedicated hardware.

ChatGPT Health rolls out widely; Gemini API adds Tool Retrieval

OpenAI is rolling out ChatGPT Health across all plan tiers and most platforms for U.S. users, including the desktop app, dots, Sites and Android, letting people connect health data across apps (Superhuman’s example: analyze sleep from a wearable, build a tracking dashboard in Sites, and have dots send proactive tips). Google added Tool Retrieval to the Gemini API: developers register functions, and the API finds and runs the right one automatically (for example, issuing a refund based on input). Also: Cloudflare published the weights of Clairic and Clairic-flash, its models for extracting specific fields from text, images and PDFs; Apple introduced FastFrames, two models that generate 480p video at interactive speed (36 ms per frame on one GPU for the smaller model); Vercel released Board, an experimental message board where AI agents claim tasks, post updates and share context; and Google introduced the Mirror Dimension, a controlled environment for testing browser-use agents without touching the live web.

Research & Science

Replit: 32 coding agents beat one by 5x on a speed-up benchmark

On Replit’s optimization benchmark, a team of 32 Opus agents achieved an 18.9x speed-up on a NumPy workload within two hours, versus 3.7x for a single agent, while using more compute. The gains depended on tasks that could be split up and on shared coordination boards, and the strongest solo model’s performance declined as teams grew. Why it matters: it gives hard numbers for when ‘agent swarms’ pay off, and when they don’t.

Links: Replit

Covered by TLDR AI

Research roundup: hidden channels, an ETH claim under review, and 1,400-year lifespans

Researchers showed that sandboxed AI models on a shared inference server could still trade information in contrived tests: one model’s large request slightly shifts the server’s calculations, and the other reads the shift as a signal. UC San Diego and MIT computer scientists are facilitating an independent review of OpenAI and Anthropic’s announcement that their models refuted the Exponential Time Hypothesis. Philosopher Nick Bostrom said on the a16z Show that safely built superintelligence could push human lifespans to 1,200-1,400 years (a range he calls conservative) by eliminating aging; for scale, WHO puts global life expectancy at 73.3 years and the longest verified lifespan is 122, and Bostrom cautions his simplified model can’t say how fast AI should be developed. Opinion (essay linked by TLDR AI): AI is already massively speeding up AI research, labs aren’t ready for recursive self-improvement, and policymakers should consider pausing or pacing frontier progress.

Links: Inference-server channel · ETH review · Bostrom · AI speeding AI research

Robotics

Tau Robotics’ humanoid moves from cleaning homes to cooking burgers

Tau Robotics, known for renting out humanoids for home cleaning in San Francisco and Los Angeles, released uncut footage of its robot frying a patty, assembling a burger and cleaning up afterward, signaling ambitions beyond cleaning; Superhuman notes the robot still has a way to go. Also this week: HistoSonics’ FDA-cleared Edison System (the company is valued at $3.75B and backed by Jeff Bezos) destroys liver tumors with focused ultrasound pulses through the skin, with about 95% technical success in trials and many patients going home the same day. ACE Robotics demoed a hotel robot that navigates rooms, runs washers and dryers and delivers clean clothes using skills learned in simulation (its 30-minutes-to-18.6-seconds claim excludes washing time and is unverified). Hyundai WIA’s coffee-mug-height parking robots, which slide under a car and move it sideways so garages fit 20-50% more vehicles, drew nearly 2M views, and Photon Matrix unveiled a backyard mosquito ‘air defense’ that spots insects with LiDAR, radar and AI vision and zaps them with a blue laser.

Links: Tau Robotics · HistoSonics · ACE Robotics · Hyundai parking robots · Photon Matrix

Robot research: a 4.72 m/s soft hexapod and robots that learn from human video

A 3D-printed, electronics-free soft hexapod reaches 4.72 m/s, about 100 times faster than its 2022 version, by reusing compressed air like a compound steam engine; it runs over sand, mulch, concrete and in water but is still tethered to a compressor. SHAPE trains a vision-language-action model on first-person human video for a two-armed robot with 22-joint hands, scoring 0.80 across six tasks versus 0.63 for π0.5 and learning new tasks from human demos with no task-specific robot data.

Links: Soft hexapod · SHAPE paper

Tech

GTA VI arrives with $2.3B+ in sales and record livestreams

After 13 years and a $1B+ development budget, Rockstar’s Grand Theft Auto VI launched with more than $2.3B from preorders and launch-day sales, tying The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time as the highest-rated video game. Livestreams peaked at 7.91M concurrent viewers (3.31M on Twitch, 3.22M on YouTube), and pre-downloads set records. Why it matters: Take-Two is counting on GTA VI to help deliver up to $8.2B in fiscal 2027 net bookings. Separately, Ubisoft is laying off 166 more employees in Canada while launching Motherbrain, a studio focused on using generative AI to build games.

Links: GTA VI · Ubisoft

Space and hardware: Terran R, orbital Kubernetes, photonics and AI in fabs

Relativity finished Terran R second-stage hot-fire testing at NASA Stennis and interstage qualification as stage-one integration nears completion; it still targets a late-2026 debut, though observers expect 2027. Sophia Space’s orbital OS copied itself across processors on one Kepler satellite, then hopped an optical link to a second satellite. Detachable fiber connectors are emerging for co-packaged optics because bonded fibers can’t be reworked or tested at volume (Senko and Advantest showed one surviving 10,000 matings), and a report finds AI can find better chip-etch recipes in months, though autonomous fab control is held back by data gaps and governance. BepiColombo’s MIXS instrument returned its first X-ray images ahead of Mercury orbit insertion on Nov 21. Update on OpenAI’s chip-design work (covered Nov 19): OpenAI is also training GPT-Synopsys with Synopsys to drive EDA tools directly.

Links: Terran R · Sophia Space · Fiber connectors · AI in fabs · BepiColombo · OpenAI RTL

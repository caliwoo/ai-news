Models & Launches

Google ships Gemini 4 Neon, a mid-tier model scoring near the top

Google released Gemini 4 Neon, a faster sibling of its top-end Gemini 4 Carbon and its fourth Gemini 4 model in under two months. Neon scores 61 on Artificial Analysis’s leaderboard, edging Carbon and trailing only Claude Fable 5.2 and GPT-6.2 Astra; Google’s own tests have it beating Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.2 Sol on 16 of 19 benchmarks, with the widest leads in long-document work, office automation and cyber tasks. It has a 1-million-token context window and four thinking levels, costs $3/$15 per million input/output tokens ($0.15 cached), a quarter below Opus 5.5 and full-price Carbon, and is live for Gemini Pro and Ultra users, the Gemini API, enterprise products and Antigravity. The night before launch, Google researcher Minkai Xu posted that “many people are surprised by how fast RSI moves,” hinting that recursive self-improvement may be behind Google’s pace. Why it matters: after a slow 2026, Google is now shipping frontier-class models in rapid succession. Opinion (The Rundown): “Google suddenly feels very, very back.” A separate analysis (TLDR Dev) projects Neon near Opus 5.5 but stresses it is a speed-based estimate with wide uncertainty, and another traced Carbon’s long runtimes to slow inference and unnecessary investigation steps.

Links: The Rundown · Google blog · Artificial Analysis · TestingCatalog · Neon vs Opus projection · Why Carbon is slow

Update: GPT-6.2 and Microsoft Ignite turn the AI race into a price war

New details on two launches covered yesterday. GPT-6.2 Astra costs the same as Fable 5.2 ($8/$40 per million input/output tokens) but twice as much for cached input ($0.40 vs $0.20); Sol, roughly as capable as Anthropic’s second-tier Opus, is priced like third-tier Sonnet ($2/$10, $0.10 cached). On Agent’s Last Exam, Astra set a high of 78.3%, 5.2 points above GPT-6 Astra at under a third of the cost and 5.5 points above Fable 5.2 at 5% of the token cost; it leads OpenAI’s GDPval v2 using 22% fewer output tokens, while Sol lands 0.5 points below Astra on Cognition’s FrontierCode 2.0 at about a fifth of the cost. At Ignite, Microsoft claimed (citing GigaOm) that Copilot is 84% cheaper than Claude and 63% cheaper than ChatGPT for average users; launched Microsoft-Decision-1.1, a decision model it says is 10x faster and 200x cheaper than traditional LLMs, now powering its Auto model router; said MAI Code 2 reaches 98% of GPT-6.1 Sol’s coding quality at about 40% of the price; and unveiled Copilot Workspaces (shared spaces for people and agents) and Agent 365 Business Analytics, which ties token spend to completed work to measure ROI. Opinion (The Deep View): models are becoming commoditized, and OpenAI needs routing and orchestration to avoid competing almost solely on price.

Links: Deep View on GPT-6.2 · Deep View on Ignite · Microsoft AI models · Decision-1.1 · Superhuman Ignite recap

Mistral bets on sovereign AI for industrial engineering

Mistral announced it is building models for industrial engineering and other regulated sectors (semiconductors, manufacturing, automotive, aerospace, energy), giving customers frontier “sovereign” models they fully control and can customize. The work builds on Mistral Large 4 (“Le Chonk”), trained in Mistral’s own European data centers, and on its Emmi AI acquisition; no timeline was given. CEO Arthur Mensch: engineering “must run on proprietary data, specialized models and systems no one else can switch off.” Why it matters: Mistral is stepping away from the price war toward customers who want to own their models. Opinion (The Deep View): there is a sizable market for Mistral, even if it won’t match Anthropic’s or OpenAI’s scale.

Business & Funding

Anthropic’s IPO raises $81.3 billion, the largest offering on record

Anthropic sold about 980 million shares at $83 each on Wednesday, raising $81.3 billion, topping SpaceX’s offering and giving it a fully diluted market value of around $1.5 trillion. Earlier this week it had guided pricing to the top of a $70 to $78 range, so the final price landed above it. Shares are expected to begin trading on the Nasdaq on Thursday under the ticker ‘ANT’; OpenAI’s IPO will likely come in 2027. On the eve of the listing, the Center for AI Safety published a statement saying Anthropic is “not a safe AI company,” signed by Nobel laureates, xAI co-founder Igor Babuschkin and Hugging Face co-founder Thomas Wolf. Why it matters: it is the biggest public test yet of investor appetite for frontier AI labs.

Links: CNBC · Pricing guidance · CAIS statement

Update: Nvidia posts $115.8B quarter as the board rules out Huang’s return

New since yesterday’s coverage of Huang’s statement: Nvidia reported Q3 revenue of $115.8 billion, with data center revenue up 110% to $107.6 billion as Vera Rubin NVL72 racks ship to CoreWeave, Google Cloud, Azure, Oracle and Nebius. Q4 guidance is $130 billion (plus or minus 3%), assuming no China data center revenue, with gross margin dipping to 71-72%. Board chair Harvey Jones said on the call the board does not intend to reinstate Huang and a search firm is leading the CEO hunt; a board source told the Washington Post there are no plans to bring him back, and one senator called the idea among the worst he’d heard. Nvidia formed an executive committee to oversee export-control compliance, Huang will testify before Congress on December 1, and Superhuman reports a new SEC probe. At her first all-hands, interim CEO Colette Kress told staff “let’s move forward” and moved pay rises and annual reviews up from March to December; staff appear divided on Huang’s return.

Robotics & Autonomy

Waymo halts freeway rides in all 15 cities after a California DMV order

California’s DMV restricted Waymo’s freeway operations on November 11 after three robotaxis entered an active construction closure on US-101 near Burlingame and another incident involved CHP vehicles on Highway 280; the DMV called it an imminent hazard, since similar incidents in May had already triggered a restriction. Waymo halted freeway service in every market while it evaluates; surface-street rides (over half a million trips a week) continue, and the restriction stays until the DMV sees effective corrective action. Opinion (essay in TLDR): Waymo can only shift road-safety policy from government to an unaccountable corporation.

Links: TechCrunch · Waymo safety essay

Research & Science

Research roundup

OpenAI says its models wrote chip-design (RTL) improvements for its Jalapeño chip and AMD designs, with up to 14% gains in power, performance and area over heavily optimized GPU baselines. Two experiments show frontier models (Claude Fable 5.2, GPT-6 Astra) reading and writing neural-network weights directly. Horizon, an Opus 5.5-based agent given a blank Hugging Face account, used 600+ GPU hours to create 38 models downloaded 50,000+ times. A 125-qubit chip teleported an error-corrected qubit over a weaker entanglement link than previously required. Researchers sent a binary message using gravity, detected by a hand-held gravimeter, as a practically unblockable channel. Marvell and Penguin showed a photonic memory appliance pooling 16 TB of DDR5 and 1 TB of HBM across 16 servers (due second half of next year). FrontierHarness Eval found harness choice alone can change coding-agent cost per task up to 5x, and Redwood Research argues third-party auditors should measure how much compute labs spend on capabilities.

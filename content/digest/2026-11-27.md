## Research & Science

### An OpenAI-assisted paper claims a path to quantum gravity, and physicists push back

Stanford and OpenAI physicist Venkatesa Chandrasekaran released an 83-page preprint proposing a way to describe gravity with quantum mechanics, saying GPT-6 Astra helped with several calculations and proofs over months and ~400M tokens. It uses the paths light takes through spacetime, covers only empty space (no matter), and claims to avoid the infinities that break standard gravity math while matching known black-hole results and general relativity. Sam Altman praised the work; UCL's Jonathan Oppenheim said AI flagged over a dozen issues, and Columbia's Peter Woit called it a publicity stunt. TLDR's linked essay concludes that, for now, AI has not solved quantum gravity. Why it matters: if it held up, it would give physicists tools for black holes and the early universe; it remains unreviewed.

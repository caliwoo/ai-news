OpenAI fixes a GPT-6.2 billing undercount as analysts see its models reasoning less

OpenAI said it corrected a billing error that had undercounted GPT-6.2 usage, so users will now hit limits 40% faster on GPT-6.2 Astra and 25% faster on Sol (The Rundown AI). Separately, an analysis argues OpenAI ‘may be in cost control mode’: in a benchmark of head-to-head work tasks, Astra 6.2 and Sol 6.2 used significantly fewer output and reasoning tokens per task relative to rivals than earlier versions did. TLDR Dev notes the benchmark does not establish OpenAI’s intent. Why it matters: usage limits on the new models effectively tighten, and the token data points to a push toward lower inference costs.

U.S. chip and distillation curbs are slowing China’s top AI labs

Reuters reports, citing multiple sources, that Zhipu AI’s planned ~3T-parameter GLM-5.5 (due this month) has been partly held up by tighter anti-distillation safeguards at OpenAI and Anthropic, and Moonshot’s next flagship Kimi K4 could slip to mid-2027 as its six-month release cycle stretches to a year. Moonshot is eyeing compute from Europe and local chipmakers and redirecting distillation toward new Google and Meta models; Kimi K3.1 Pro slid from fourth to ninth as Artificial Analysis moved to private tests (The Rundown AI). The driver: a rule announced Nov. 16 closing the loophole that let Chinese firms rent Nvidia’s best chips in overseas data centers. Chip sellers are telling neoclouds they can’t buy Nvidia chips if any capacity will be rented by Chinese companies, adding pressure on neocloud stocks (TLDR, via CNBC). Why it matters: China has been the main source of strong open-weight models; The Rundown says that supply could thin just as demand grows.

Links: The Rundown · CNBC

SAP confirms a cyberattack on its software development systems

SAP is investigating an attack on its software development infrastructure after detecting suspicious activity on Thursday, and says it found no evidence customer business data was accessed. Attackers reportedly stole employees’ authentication tokens and used them to copy and alter source code on GitHub (not confirmed by SAP). Why it matters: no software bug was needed, stolen developer credentials were enough. There’s no patch yet; The Code advises reviewing how you protect developer tokens and signing keys.

Research & Science

An OpenAI-assisted paper claims a path to quantum gravity, and physicists push back

Stanford and OpenAI physicist Venkatesa Chandrasekaran released an 83-page preprint proposing a way to describe gravity with quantum mechanics, saying GPT-6 Astra helped with several calculations and proofs over months and ~400M tokens. It uses the paths light takes through spacetime, covers only empty space (no matter), and claims to avoid the infinities that break standard gravity math while matching known black-hole results and general relativity. Sam Altman praised the work; UCL’s Jonathan Oppenheim said AI flagged over a dozen issues, and Columbia’s Peter Woit called it a publicity stunt. TLDR’s linked essay concludes that, for now, AI has not solved quantum gravity. Why it matters: if it held up, it would give physicists tools for black holes and the early universe; it remains unreviewed.

Links: The Rundown · ‘It’s so over for quantum gravity?’

Caltech’s light-powered ‘BrainBots’ carry chemo into mouse brains

Near-infrared light propels 250nm carriers that bind to cells transporting them across the blood–brain barrier; eight hours after dosing, activated BrainBots delivered 5.2x as much doxorubicin to brain tissue as unactivated carriers. In mice with glioblastoma, treatment slowed tumor growth and extended survival with less systemic toxicity. Oxford’s Tim Viney told the FT the evidence needed ‘significant improvement’ and questioned translation to humans.

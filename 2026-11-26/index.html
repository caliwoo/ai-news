Research & Science

OpenAI claims the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly 9/4 as AI math results keep coming

OpenAI posted a preprint arguing that the exponent of square matrix multiplication (ω) is exactly 9/4 over every field; per TLDR AI, a smaller exponent would violate a lower bound for testing whether entries in several independent lists are distinct. The Rundown says it was one of nine more AI-written math papers OpenAI released to GitHub, while Google posted six new math papers from Cogentic, its Gemini 4-powered research agent. Separately, 16-year-old student researcher Cinna Davis said she solved 100+ open math conjectures with GPT-6.2 Astra; her proofs are on GitHub but not yet verified. Why it matters: lower bounds are rarely proven, so this is a notable claim if it holds up. Opinion (Scott Aaronson, via TLDR and TLDR Dev): labs are now reporting matching upper and lower bounds for problems like matrix multiplication and the Fourier transform, and a proof of P ≠ NP ‘may soon be within reach.’

Links: Preprint · Scott Aaronson · The Rundown · Google’s Cogentic papers · Cinna Davis

OpenAI says GPT-6 models can design proteins with reasoning and code alone

OpenAI published research showing its GPT-6-class models can design new proteins using only reasoning and code, without calling specialized biology models; The Rundown says they matched or beat biology-specific AI models. A research partner’s lab tests confirmed some designs worked, though TLDR AI notes the work still relied on specialist tools to screen candidates. Why it matters: general-purpose models are moving into territory once reserved for purpose-built scientific AI.

Agent swarms: faster, but not yet smarter

Two pieces landed the same day. Hugging Face grew a swarm to 1,000 DeepSeek agents over nine days to build PyBot, a pure-Python chatbot that matched SmolLM-360M on everyday conversations; the run produced 175,000 messages and more than 8,500 merged pull requests, and exposed bottlenecks in inference, tool calls and Git (TLDR Dev). Meanwhile Paradigm3’s ‘Swarms do not yet raise the roof’ study found that at equal token budgets swarms deliver results faster but less efficiently than single agents, with a quality penalty that grows with swarm size; earlier ‘swarm premiums’ came from single agents going stale, and with fresh context resets single agents win (TLDR AI). Why it matters: swarms mostly buy speed, not capability, for now.

Links: Hugging Face · Paradigm3 study

Research notes: looped layers, faster cache translation, fewer SQL errors

Looped transformers (as in Inkling 2 Mini and GLM-5.4) rerun some layers per token: in a small test, going from one to six passes raised the longest chain solved at 90% accuracy from 7 to 36 hops, with little effect on factual recall (TLDR Dev). Ramp Labs trained one translator that maps hidden states across 42 directions between seven models, so a receiving model can build its key-value cache without rereading the text; on 32,000-token documents it began answering 4x faster while keeping over 90% of accuracy (TLDR Dev). Grain-aware hints cut text-to-SQL ‘fan-out’ errors on the BIRD benchmark from 18.4% to about 13% (TLDR Data).

Links: Looped transformers · Ramp Labs · Text-to-SQL paper

Business & Work

Anthropic: the biggest AI gains come from reorganizing work, not cutting staff

Anthropic published research from interviews with 58 of its U.S. enterprise customers covering about 300 AI use cases across finance, real estate, medicine, engineering, logistics and media. Key findings: the largest use case is extending coding agents and software development to nontechnical roles, adding output rather than cutting cost; AI hasn’t caused significant layoffs but is slowing hiring and reducing the need for contractors, ‘particularly offshore ones’; bottlenecks are employee resistance and tasks AI hasn’t sped up yet (code review, sales, decision-making); top-down automation fuels anxiety while bottom-up adoption is irregular; and the most value comes from ‘meaningful reorganization’ of roles. Microsoft Azure CTO Amir Netz told The Deep View that employees who don’t use AI may be at a ‘massive disadvantage.’ Opinion (The Deep View / AI for Work): AI deployments only work if workers are on board, which takes education, enthusiasm and job security rather than pressure and fearmongering.

Links: Anthropic research · AI for Work

Cybersecurity

AI agents found and exploited a zero-day with almost no human input, Amazon says

Amazon said AI agents running on Cairn, an open-source penetration-testing framework, found and exploited a zero-day in a medical imaging AI app on a university server with no human oversight beyond a goal prompt. Why it matters: Amazon’s message is that agentic offensive operations are already happening and defenders must plan for their speed and persistence.

Models & Launches

Amazon joins the ‘decision model’ race with Strands Decider v2

AWS launched Strands Decider v2, five open decision models built on Google’s Gemma 4, ranging from 2B to 31B parameters. Like Typesafe’s Jev, they don’t write human-readable text; they return a probability for each of up to 255 developer-supplied answers, suited to yes/no questions, classification and scoring (e.g., which tool to call or where to route a request). New versus the first release seven weeks ago: shared context processing (one document processed once for many questions) and bidirectional attention; Amazon says the 31B model beats competitors on decision, multimodal, video and e-commerce tasks. They’re self-hosted for now, not on Bedrock. Hugging Face’s index counts 345 ‘open reproductions’ of Jev, and TLDR Dev links a Modaic analysis inferring Jev likely runs on Qwen3.5-35B-A3B. Opinion (The Deep View): cheap decision models could take a big chunk out of frontier labs’ token revenue.

Links: Announcement · Decision model index · Modaic on Jev

Opinion: ‘The harness matters more than the model’

Dharmesh Shah (Simple AI) argues the software around a model, its context, tools, skills and guardrails, drives results more than the model itself: the model is the engine, the harness is the car. General-purpose harnesses like ChatGPT and Claude can be configured for specific jobs but need constant context upkeep, while purpose-built ones (his example is YouSpot, a personal CRM he works on, and HubSpot Breeze) do it for you and can plug into ChatGPT or Claude via MCP. This is the author’s opinion and he has a stake in the products named.
